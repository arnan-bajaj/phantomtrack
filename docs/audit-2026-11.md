# Audit, November 2026 (core math, robot frame and safety, tests, PR #16)

Audited commit: `108780a` (main after PR #16). Line numbers below refer to that commit.
Everything in Phase A was read-only. The evidence was produced by throwaway harnesses and Python
scripts outside the repo. Phase B fixes are on branch `audit-fixes`, one commit per bug; see
"Phase B status" at the end.

Severity scale:
- **High**: a wrong or non-finite command, or the driver's controls reversed.
- **Medium**: a documented contract is broken, or experiment numbers are not trustworthy.
- **Low**: an edge case, a documentation error, or a counter that is off.

Status values:
- **CONFIRMED**: reproduced with a concrete input.
- **UNCERTAIN**: the behavior is real, but whether it is a bug is a design question.

## Findings, most severe first

### H1. One NaN sighting poisons a Kalman track forever, and the assist outputs NaN commands. CONFIRMED

- **Where**: `src/main/java/phantom/core/tracking/KalmanOpponentTracker.java:64-84`. Measurements
  are used without checking that they are finite. `MultiTargetTracker` does check, at
  `MultiTargetTracker.java:375-382`. Nothing in `DefenseAssist.update` (`DefenseAssist.java:92-112`)
  stops a non-finite track or command.
- **Failing input**: `KalmanOpponentTracker.defaults(source, 0.1)` gets id 0 at (6, 4) at t = 0,
  0.02 and 0.04, then `(NaN, 4)` at t = 0.06. The setup is Shadow, self at (10, 4), and the assist
  is engaged.
- **Expected**: the bad sighting is dropped, and the command stays finite.
- **Actual**: from t = 0.06 on, the track is `(NaN, 4)` with velocity `(NaN, 0)`, and the command
  is `v = (NaN, NaN), omega = NaN` on every loop. Later good sightings cannot fix it, and the track
  never times out while sightings keep arriving.
- **Effect**: this breaks the CLAUDE.md rule "Never let NaN reach a command". On the robot,
  `CommandLimits.sanitize` would turn the command into STOP, so the assist would sit dead as long as
  the track lives. The bug is latent today: the robot uses `PerfectTracker` and the sim camera never
  produces NaN. It becomes live with Phase 8 vision.

### H2. On red with `Symmetry.MIRRORED`, the driver's left/right and turning are reversed. CONFIRMED

- **Where**: `robot/src/main/java/frc/robot/DefenseTeleop.java:91-112`. The stick command from
  `DriverInput.command` is treated as blue-frame. `PhantomTrack` hands it back unchanged when the
  driver drives, and `AllianceFrame.commandToReal`
  (`AllianceFrame.java:40-42` -> `FieldMirror.command`, `FieldMirror.java:63-65`) maps it to the
  real field with the field's symmetry.
- **Why it is wrong**: a red driver is a person standing at the red wall, turned 180 degrees. They
  are not a reflection of the blue driver. Their stick must always be rotated 180 degrees.
  `MIRRORED` reflects vy and omega instead.
- **Failing input**: `MIRRORED` on red. The stick is pushed left: `DriverInput` gives (0, +v),
  omega 0.
- **Expected**: the robot moves to the driver's left, which is real -y for someone facing -x.
  Turning the stick CCW should give real CCW.
- **Actual**: `commandToReal` gives (0, +v): the robot moves to the driver's right. A CCW stick
  (omega = +w) gives -w, a clockwise turn.
- `ROTATED`, the value in `Constants.MIRROR` today, is correct. The bug becomes live as soon as
  the 2027 field is mirrored (as in 2023/2024), which `Constants` tells you to set from the manual.
  It inverts the driver-override path. The ghosts also see our "desired velocity" mirrored the
  wrong way, so ghost pushing and the pin referee would be wrong too.

### M1. Shadow and Interpose put their target inside an opponent that drives away from the goal. CONFIRMED

- **Where**: `ShadowMode.java:53` and `InterposeMode.java:61`, `predicted = p_opp + v_opp * lead`.
- **Contract broken**: Shadow's `standoffMeters` is documented as a center-to-center distance chosen
  "so we are not in constant contact". Interpose says "never closer to them than minStandoff".
- **Math**: suppose the opponent moves straight away from the goal at speed |v|. Following it
  goal-side, the target is `standoff - |v| * lead` from its current center. That is below the
  0.9 m bumper contact distance for any |v| > (1.1 - 0.9) / 0.25 = 0.8 m/s.
- **Failing input (Shadow)**: `ShadowMode.defaults()` (1.1 m, 0.25 s), placeholder field, opponent
  at (8.887, 4.032) moving (-4.5, 0), which is the state at t = 4.42 s of SimTest's 30 s run.
  - Expected: target at least 0.9 m (bumpers touching) from the opponent, by design 1.1 m.
  - Actual: (8.862, 4.033), **0.025 m** from the opponent's center.
- **Failing input (Interpose)**: `InterposeMode(0.5, 1.1, 0.25)`, goal (12, 4.035), opponent at
  (10, 4.035) moving (-4, 0).
  - Expected: target at least 1.1 m from the opponent.
  - Actual: (10.5, 4.035), 0.5 m.
- **Measured (perfect tracking, 180 s)**:
  - Plain Shadow's target is inside the guarded robot for 60 s of 157 s of engaged guarding (Cyclers).
  - 57% (Cyclers) and 80% (Evaders) of plain Shadow's referee-counted pressing is on an opponent
    moving away from the goal.
  - In SimTest's run, Shadow drives into opponent #2's back at full speed from t = 3.8 s to 4.9 s.
    The pin guard then has to back off.
- This is also why SimTest's goal-side number sits near 0.75 (see T1).

### M2. `DriverBlend` ignores the driver's rotation. CONFIRMED

- **Where**: `src/main/java/phantom/core/control/DriverBlend.java:45-47`. `isOverriding` only looks
  at `|v_driver|`.
- **Contract broken**: the Javadoc says "The driver can always take over".
- **Failing input**: `DriverBlend.defaults()` (alpha 1, override 0.5). The driver commands
  (v = 0, omega = 2.0) and the assist commands omega = -1.0, with the assist engaged.
  - Expected: output omega 2.0, because the driver takes over.
  - Actual: -1.0.
- The robot's `DriverBlend(1.0, 0.0)` behaves the same way. On the robot, only `SafetyGate`
  catches turning (its deflection includes |r|), so core's contract is not kept by core itself.
- With alpha < 1, a large driver omega with little translation does not count as an override.
  It is then blended in, so the output omega can exceed `maxOmega`.

### M3. `FieldConfig.clampInside` can return a point inside an obstacle attached to a field wall. CONFIRMED (latent)

- **Where**: `FieldConfig.java:96-104` and `Obstacle.java:40-57`. `pushOut` picks the shortest
  axis even when that exit is outside the field. `clampToWalls` then pushes the point straight
  back into the obstacle.
- **Failing input**: a 10 x 10 field, robot half size 0.45, obstacle (0, 2)-(1, 6).
  `clampInside((0.45, 4))` computes left = 0.9 and right = 1.0, so it pushes left to (-0.45, 4).
  The wall clamp moves that to (0.45, 4).
  - Expected: (1.45, 4).
  - Actual: (0.45, 4), which `blocks()` is true for.
- Every mode, the escape planner and the back-off use `clampInside`. Today's three layouts are not
  affected, because their walls are thin in x and long in y, so the push toward the field wall
  never wins. Real 2027 geometry could easily be affected.

### M4. `ContactDetector.Config` accepts zero or negative ramp widths, which flips the vision cue. CONFIRMED (latent)

- **Where**: `ContactDetector.java:150-156` (`withVisionError`) and `186-189` (`ramp`).
- **Failing input**: `Config.placeholder().withVisionError(0.0)`, which means "a perfect camera".
  - Expected: `visionCue(1.0) = 0` (1 m apart is not touching) and `visionCue(0.0) = 1`.
  - Actual: `visionCue(1.0) = 1.0`, `visionCue(0.0) = 0.0`, and `visionCue(0.05) = NaN`.
- A NaN in a hypothesis never leaves it: `p += (NaN - p)...` stays NaN. It also freezes
  `inContact` at whatever it was.
- `withVisionError(-0.2)` also gives `visionCue(1.0) = 1`.
- The sim passes 0.05 m and 0.4 m, so this is latent. On the robot, "the measured tracking error"
  goes straight into this call.

### M5. Two Cyclers meeting head-on inside a gap deadlock for the rest of the run (sim). CONFIRMED, not fixed

- **Where**: `src/main/java/phantom/sim/CyclerBehavior.java:142-147`. The stuck-at-gate rule only
  runs while the straight path is blocked. Inside the gap it is not blocked, so `gateStuckFor` is
  reset every loop and the "treat that gate as blocked for 3 s" rule never fires. Cyclers do not
  run `evade`, so their bulldoze timer never runs either.
- **Failing input**: two lanes, no defense, Cyclers, 180 s, dt = 10 ms. This is the same code with
  `SimSession.DT = 0.01` and the drive-telemetry sample period at 0.01.
  - Expected: about 24 cycles each, as at 20 ms.
  - Actual: opponents #0 and #2 stop at (8.41, 2.12) and (9.31, 2.22) from t of about 27 s until
    the end of the run, with 3 and 4 cycles.
- **Timestep dependence**: at 20 ms this exact meeting does not happen in the baselines. The
  mechanism is the same, though, as the "two-lane gridlock" and "partners jam the lanes" notes in
  README.
- **Metric**: the guarded opponent's "Slowed" uses the mean of *completed* cycles
  (`CyclerBehavior.java:233-235`). The jammed baseline therefore reads 7.50 s. Only the Total and
  all-opponent columns show it, where it appears as -51%.
- **Not fixed**: the fix is a sim-behavior design decision: who backs out of a head-on stall, and
  whether that also applies against our robot. See "Not fixed".

### L1. The pin guard's 3 s back-off timeout never ends a back-off. It restarts it and counts it again. CONFIRMED

- **Where**: `RulesGuard.java:364-391`. A back-off older than `MAX_BACKOFF_SECONDS` is removed, but
  its episode is still open and still over the threshold. The trigger loop in the same `apply`
  call therefore re-adds it and increments `backoffsTriggered`.
- **Contract broken**: the Javadoc says it backs off "until ... or MAX_BACKOFF_SECONDS pass".
- **Failing input**: the `CrowdEscapeTest` corner (two stopped opponents touching, nothing safe),
  run to 6.0 s.
  - Expected: 2 back-offs (one per opponent), as in `cornerWithNoSafeExitYieldsThenSlidesOut`.
  - Actual: one extra back-off per opponent every 3 s.
- **Measured**: 6 of 952 back-offs in the headless grid (3 layouts x 2 opponent kinds x 16 setups)
  are such restarts. The behavior itself (keep backing off while the episode is over the threshold)
  is the safe choice. The documentation and the counter are what is wrong.

### L2. The referee drops the first loop of every touch. CONFIRMED (documented convention, small)

- **Where**: `PinTimer.java:96-116`. "The first loop of a touch adds nothing", so an episode made of
  N separate touches is under-counted by N * dt.
- **Measured** over the headless grid against true pressing time (gap <= 0.05 m and effort > 0.1,
  summed per episode):
  - The worst under-count in one episode is 0.50 s, in a no-guard row that is a pin anyway.
  - In rows with the guard on, it is 0.22 s at most.
  - The largest true pressing time in a guarded episode is 1.98 s, so no pin is hidden.
- A contact that flickers on and off every loop would count 0. Nothing in the grid does that.

### L3. `EpisodeKeys` remembers the first stale position, not "the last reported position". CONFIRMED

- **Where**: `EpisodeKeys.java:200-202`. With no fresh sighting yet, `lastPos` is only written when
  the key has no entry.
- **Failing input**: id 5 is reported only stale (age > 0.25 s), at (2, 2) and then at (2.9, 2).
  A new id appears at (3.85, 2) while id 5 is stale.
  - Expected (per the Javadoc): it takes over, because it is 0.95 m from the last reported
    (2.9, 2).
  - Actual: it gets its own key, because it is 1.85 m from the first (2, 2).

### L4. The search heading is not wrapped. CONFIRMED

- **Where**: `DefenseAssist.java:151`. `heading = angle(d) + 0.6 sin(0.8 t)` reaches 3.7416 rad on
  the placeholder field. This breaks CLAUDE.md rule 6, that angles are in (-pi, pi]. The
  controller wraps the error, so the motion is right, but anything that reads
  `DefenseTarget.heading` (telemetry, logs) sees an out-of-range angle.
- **Failing input**: `searchTarget(t)` with sin(0.8 t) = 1, i.e. t = pi / 1.6.
  - Expected: wrap(pi + 0.6) = -2.5416.
  - Actual: 3.7416.

### L5. `Vec2.angle()` returns -pi for (x < 0, y = -0.0). CONFIRMED

- **Where**: `Vec2.java:70`. `Math.atan2(-0.0, -1)` is -pi.
- **Failing input**: `new Vec2(1, 0).times(-1).angle()`.
  - Expected: pi, since the range is documented as (-pi, pi].
  - Actual: -3.141592653589793.

### L6. Wrong number in a hand-computed comment. CONFIRMED (documentation)

- **Where**: `src/test/java/phantom/core/rules/IdSwitchPinTest.java:35, 109, 137` give
  `0.6193746 + (0.8175745 - 0.6193746) * 0.7575758 = 0.7695266`.
- The correct value is **0.7695260**. Nothing asserts it, and it is above 0.7 either way.

### U1. Hold ground pushes on the back of an opponent that is leaving. UNCERTAIN (design)

- **Where**: `HoldGroundMode.java:73-82`. "Hold" fires when we are goal-side and within 0.3 m,
  whichever way the opponent is moving.
- **Measured**: 12-69% of the referee-counted pressing in the "+ hold" rows is on an opponent moving
  away from the goal. That is pin exposure with no defensive value. The README describes hold as
  "stops retreating and pushes straight back", which does not cover pushing a robot that is leaving.
- Whether hold should require a closing opponent is a design decision. It is listed here, and not
  fixed.

### Info (no change proposed)

- `RulesGuard.lastSeen` is never pruned. It grows by one entry per episode key, a few hundred per
  match with anonymous tracking.
- `RulesGuard.LOOP_SECONDS = 0.02` is a constant in the margin rule, which is conservative at
  10 ms.
- `ContactDetector` never reads `DriveTelemetry.timestampSec`. A repeated (stale) sample is used as
  new. `SimSession` would do that if `DT` were smaller than the telemetry sample period.
- On the robot, the pin timers run on ghost-world time, which is clamped to `MAX_DT = 0.1 s` per
  loop. A loop overrun therefore under-counts real time. This is consistent with the ghost referee,
  but real opponents later need FPGA time.
- `ContactDetector`'s smoothing `p += (raw - p) dt / (tau + dt)` is backward Euler. Its rise time
  depends a little on dt (0.7 is reached after 40 ms at both 10 and 20 ms in the test case). It is
  stable for every dt.

## The referee metric and pins

- The "referee contact" time (`SimWorld.countPins`, `PinTimer.update` with `pressing`) counts only
  loops where the bumper gap is at most 0.05 m **and** `Contact.effort(desiredVelocity, toward
  opponent, maxSpeed) > 0.1`. That is: we command at least 10% of top speed (0.45 m/s in the sim,
  0.1 m/s at the Phase 7 1 m/s cap) toward the opponent. Being pushed (effort <= 0), backing away
  (< 0) and yielding (v = 0) do not count. This matches the README.
- A pin is one per episode whose referee time reaches 3.0 s. An episode ends only at 1.83 m apart.
  This is `pinViolations()`, as the README says.
- Caveats:
  - "Pressing" is judged from the commanded velocity, not from force.
  - Each touch loses one loop (L2).
  - Total "Contact" in the headless table is any touch, including being pushed, which is not
    referee time.

## PR #16: "one ghost is fixed / not worse"

The current `PinMarginStudy` was re-run at `108780a` and against `51806c9`, the commit before #16.
The harness only needed a stub for the new `guardYielding()` and an accessor. Both runs reproduce
the PR's tables exactly. Per setup, over the 24 one-ghost setups:

- **Peak contact per episode (the pin metric): the claim holds.**
  - 2.98 -> 0.40 s and 2.56 -> 2.48 s.
  - Two setups rose by 0.02 s (0.42 -> 0.44 and 1.40 -> 1.42).
  - Referee peak rose by 0.02 s in two setups.
  - All 24 are now at most 2.5 s (2.48 s max).
- **Total time in contact: worse, and not mentioned.** All four 1.0 m/s Evader setups touched the
  ghost 38-58% longer (16.9 -> 23.5 s, 15.9 -> 24.0 s, 18.1 -> 25.0 s, 16.4 -> 25.9 s).
- **Slowed: one setup lost all its effect** (0.5 m/s Evader, Line mirror + hold: +17% -> +0%). The PR
  mentions this. Two others went +0% -> +8%.
- Each setup is a single deterministic 300 s run. One ghost cycle is about 8 points of "slowed", so
  ±8 is one cycle.
- **Judgement**: merging was defensible for the stated goal, which was peak contact at most 2.5 s
  with one ghost. "Not worse" overstates it:
  - It is not worse on per-episode peaks.
  - It is worse on total contact exposure in 4 of 24 setups, and on effect in 1 of 24.
  - The evidence is one run per setup.

## Timestep (10 ms vs 20 ms)

The same code was run with `SimSession.DT = 0.01` and the drive-telemetry period at 0.01, using a
copy outside the repo.

- With the pin guard on, pins stay 0 in every headless table.
- Slowed % moves by up to about ±5 points. For example, Shadow vs Cyclers +6% -> +9%, and Cut-off
  vs Cyclers +32% -> +36%.
- Rows without the guard change a lot (Shadow + hold, no guard, vs Cyclers: 9 pins -> 1). These are
  chaotic pushing matches.
- The large difference is M5: the two-lane no-defense baseline gridlocks at 10 ms.
- In core, the only dt-dependent constants are `LOOP_SECONDS` (conservative) and the detector's
  smoothing (see Info). `PinTimer` loses one dt per touch (L2).
